# Opening scenes: design audit (13 Nov 2026)

The eight Signature openings (Rosewood Doors, Once Upon a Time, Barn & Eucalyptus, Garden Gate, Shoreline,
Satin Bow, Opening Night, Lanterns), checked frame by frame at phone size: closed, half a second after the tap,
and mid-opening. Benchmark: the premium animated invitations couples see elsewhere. We take the bar (rich
materials, light, depth, a reveal that feels like an event), never their artwork, names or layouts.

## Verdict

They read as clip art, not as a luxury product. Three causes run through all eight:

1. **Flat drawing with a filter on top.** Every surface is a vector shape with one "bevel and emboss" filter
   lit from the same corner. That is the 2005 Photoshop look: plastic, muddy, no real material. Wood has no
   grain depth, velvet no sheen, satin no highlight roll, iron no metal.
2. **No light, no depth.** One flat colour field per scene. Nothing casts a shadow, nothing catches light,
   nothing sits in front of anything else. Real premium openings are lit like a photograph: a warm key light,
   soft falloff, contact shadows, a glow spilling through the opening.
3. **The animation is a slide, not a moment.** Each scene does one CSS move (a swing, a slide, a fade) on one
   ease, then cuts to the card. No anticipation, no camera, no follow-through, nothing behind the opening (a
   blank colour, a white haze or a cartoon hill), and the card arrives separately instead of being revealed.

## Scene by scene

| Scene | Closed | Opening | Worst problem |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rosewood Doors | Muddy maroon, pompom "dahlias" on noodle stems, brushed-metal grain on painted doors, peach seal, the seal's letters double-printed (blurry) | Doors turn like flat cards: no thickness, no light through the gap, behind them a blank pink page | Cheap emboss; empty reveal |
| Once Upon a Time | A small navy rectangle on lavender, clip-art gold castle, the guest's name runs over the book's bottom edge | Cover flips to a children's-book page: lilac castle on a cartoon hill; "Once upon a time" clipped by the cover | Childish, not fairytale-luxe |
| Barn & Eucalyptus | Flat vector planks, see-through X braces, a sticker wreath with a tiny tag, a black bar like a garage handle | Wreath and tag are cut in half down the seam; behind: cartoon hill, white rectangles for chairs | Wreath split; clip-art reveal |
| Garden Gate | The gate covers the whole screen in repeated black curls; name text sits on the bars (hard to read) | Gates squash flat as they turn; fountain clip-art; white dots for flowers | Busy, heavy, unreadable |
| Shoreline | Stock gradient sky, blurred sun, flat sea band, horizon dead centre, initials and heart drawn flat on the sand | The "wave" is a pale band wiping up the screen: reads as fog, not water | Not a wave |
| Satin Bow | Cold grey; embossed rosettes look like stains on wallpaper; plastic bow | The bow fades away instead of untying; ribbon pieces jump off-screen as loose squares; the vertical ribbon slides across the couple's names | Glitches over the names |
| Opening Night | Best of the eight, but stripes not folds, thin pelmet, tiny fringe, tiny initials | Curtains slide, behind them a white haze, no stage, no light | Empty reveal |
| Lanterns | Most atmospheric; lanterns are orange blobs, too saturated, too big for the trees | Lanterns rise and vanish; nothing lands | Lanterns read as stickers |

Shared details:

- The guest's name and "Tap to open" sit over the busiest part of every scene, with a weak shadow.
- The seal's initials are drawn twice (a shadow copy offset), which reads as a printing error.
- The live sample's seal says "H & T" while the sample couple has other names.
- The front page shows five of them opening at once in small phones: the weakest look worst when small.

## How we do it better, our way

1. **Fewer, finished.** Four scenes done properly beat eight that look cheap: Doors, Curtain, Lanterns, Bow.
   Book, Barn, Gate and Beach come off the front page until each meets the same bar (kept for couples who
   chose them).
2. **Light first.** Each scene gets a key light (warm, top left), a soft vignette, contact shadows under
   raised detail, a specular rim on metal and satin, and a glow behind the opening that grows as it opens.
3. **Real materials.** Lacquered panels with a soft highlight band instead of brushed grain; velvet as
   folds (alternating light and shadow, never stripes); satin with a moving highlight; a wax seal with a
   pooled edge, a glossy highlight and pressed (not printed) letters.
4. **Choreography.** Every opening becomes one short film (about 2.8 s):
   tap → a small press (anticipation, 120 ms) → the seal cracks or the bow loosens → the camera pushes in a
   little as the doors part → warm light floods out → petals or motes drift through → the card rises out of
   the light, sharpening as it comes → the scene clears. Eases with overshoot and settle, never one linear
   move.
5. **The reveal lands on the card.** Behind every opening is the card itself, lit, never a blank colour or a
   cartoon landscape.
6. **Words stay readable.** The name and "Tap to open" sit on a soft dark scrim at the foot of the scene, in
   the invitation's own script, with a slow breathing glow.
7. **Our own art only.** Everything drawn by us in code, from our own shapes: no traced motifs, fonts or
   names from anyone else.
